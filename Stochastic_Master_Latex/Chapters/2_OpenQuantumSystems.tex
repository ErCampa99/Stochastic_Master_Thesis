\section{Continuously monitored quantum systems}

In many relevant experimental scenarios a quantum system is not only affected by an environment,
but the environment is also \emph{continuously measured}. In this case the system state is updated
conditioned on the measurement record, and the evolution becomes stochastic.
The unconditional (ensemble-averaged) dynamics is still described by a Markovian master equation,
while the conditional dynamics is described by a \emph{stochastic master equation} (SME).

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{From system--environment interaction to Lindblad master equation}

We consider a system $S$ interacting with an environment $B$, and assume that the interaction can
be described as a sequence of short collisions between $S$ and independent environmental modes.
This viewpoint leads naturally to a Markovian dynamics in the continuous-time limit.
We outline here a minimal derivation of a Lindblad master equation in the spirit of collision models,
closely following Ref.

Let $\rho(t)$ be the system density operator at time $t$.
In an infinitesimal step $dt$ the system interacts unitarily with an environmental mode initially
prepared in a reference state (vacuum) $\ket{0}_t$, so that the joint state evolves as
\begin{equation}
	\rho(t)\otimes \ket{0}_t\!\bra{0}
	\ \longrightarrow\
	U(t,t+dt)\big(\rho(t)\otimes \ket{0}_t\!\bra{0}\big)U^\dagger(t,t+dt).
\end{equation}
Tracing out the environmental mode gives the reduced update
\begin{equation}
	\rho(t+dt)
	=
	\Tr_B\!\left[
	U(t,t+dt)\big(\rho(t)\otimes \ket{0}_t\!\bra{0}\big)U^\dagger(t,t+dt)
	\right].
	\label{eq:uncond_update_traceB}
\end{equation}

Assume that the interaction is such that, to first order in $dt$, the reduced map can be written in a Kraus form
with two types of operators: a \emph{no-jump} operator $M_0$ and a \emph{jump} operator $M_1$,
\begin{equation}
	\rho(t+dt)= M_0\,\rho(t)\,M_0^\dagger + M_1\,\rho(t)\,M_1^\dagger,
	\qquad
	M_0^\dagger M_0 + M_1^\dagger M_1=\mathbb{I}+O(dt^2).
	\label{eq:Kraus_uncond}
\end{equation}
A standard choice consistent with the continuous-time limit is
\begin{equation}
	M_1=\sqrt{\kappa\,dt}\,c,
	\qquad
	M_0=\mathbb{I}-iH\,dt-\frac{\kappa}{2}\,c^\dagger c\,dt,
	\label{eq:Kraus_choice_jump}
\end{equation}
where $H$ is the system Hamiltonian and $c$ is the system coupling (collapse) operator.

Inserting Eq.~\eqref{eq:Kraus_choice_jump} into Eq.~\eqref{eq:Kraus_uncond} and expanding to first order in $dt$,
we obtain
\begin{align}
	\rho(t+dt)-\rho(t)
	&=
	-i[H,\rho(t)]\,dt
	+\kappa\,c\rho(t)c^\dagger\,dt
	-\frac{\kappa}{2}\{c^\dagger c,\rho(t)\}\,dt
	+O(dt^2).
\end{align}
Dividing by $dt$ and taking the limit $dt\to 0$ yields the Lindblad master equation
\begin{equation}
	\frac{d\rho(t)}{dt}
	=
	-i[H,\rho(t)]
	+\kappa\,\mathcal{D}[c]\rho(t),
	\qquad
	\mathcal{D}[A]\rho=A\rho A^\dagger-\frac{1}{2}\{A^\dagger A,\rho\}.
	\label{eq:Lindblad_single_channel}
\end{equation}
The generalization to multiple channels $\{c_k\}$ with rates $\{\kappa_k\}$ is immediate:
\begin{equation}
	\frac{d\rho(t)}{dt}
	=
	-i[H,\rho(t)]
	+\sum_k \kappa_k\,\mathcal{D}[c_k]\rho(t).
	\label{eq:Lindblad_multi_channel}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Conditional states and unravellings}

A Lindblad master equation describes the \emph{unconditional} evolution of the system state,
i.e.\ the evolution averaged over all possible measurement outcomes of the environment.
If instead the environment is continuously monitored, the system state becomes conditioned on the
measurement record.
We denote the conditional state by $\rho^{(c)}(t)$, and the unconditional state is recovered by averaging:
\begin{equation}
	\rho(t)=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\rho^{(c)}(t)\right].
	\label{eq:uncond_as_average}
\end{equation}

The representation of the same unconditional dynamics in terms of stochastic conditional evolutions is called
an \emph{unravelling} of the master equation.
Unravellings are not unique.
Physically, different unravellings correspond to different continuous measurements performed on the output
modes of the environment (e.g.\ photodetection, homodyne, heterodyne).
Mathematically, this non-uniqueness is related to the freedom in choosing measurement operators, and to the
unitary freedom in the Lindblad operators describing the same GKSL generator.

In what follows we focus on the paradigmatic jump-like unravelling associated with continuous photodetection,
as presented in Ref.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\subsection{Photodetection: click and no-click updates, and the jump SME}

Consider first a single monitored channel with collapse operator $c$ and rate $\kappa$.
In a short interval $dt$, photodetection yields two possible outcomes:
``no click'' (outcome $0$) or ``click'' (outcome $1$).
These outcomes are described by the Kraus operators in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Kraus_choice_jump}.
The (unnormalized) conditional states after the two outcomes are
\begin{align}
	\tilde\rho^{(c)}_0(t+dt)
	&= M_0\,\rho^{(c)}(t)\,M_0^\dagger,
	\label{eq:unnorm_no_click}\\
	\tilde\rho^{(c)}_1(t+dt)
	&= M_1\,\rho^{(c)}(t)\,M_1^\dagger
	= \kappa\,dt\; c\,\rho^{(c)}(t)\,c^\dagger.
	\label{eq:unnorm_click}
\end{align}
The corresponding probabilities are given by the traces:
\begin{align}
	p_1(t+dt)
	&=\Tr\!\left[\tilde\rho^{(c)}_1(t+dt)\right]
	=\kappa\,\Tr\!\left[c^\dagger c\,\rho^{(c)}(t)\right]dt
	=\kappa\,\langle c^\dagger c\rangle_t\,dt,
	\label{eq:prob_click}\\
	p_0(t+dt)
	&=\Tr\!\left[\tilde\rho^{(c)}_0(t+dt)\right]
	=1-p_1(t+dt)+O(dt^2).
	\label{eq:prob_noclick}
\end{align}
The normalized conditional states are then
\begin{equation}
	\rho^{(c)}_{0,1}(t+dt)=\frac{\tilde\rho^{(c)}_{0,1}(t+dt)}{\Tr[\tilde\rho^{(c)}_{0,1}(t+dt)]}.
\end{equation}

\subsubsection*{No-click update to first order in $dt$}

Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:Kraus_choice_jump} and expanding Eq.~\eqref{eq:unnorm_no_click} to first order in $dt$, we obtain
\begin{align}
	\tilde\rho^{(c)}_0(t+dt)
	&=
	\rho^{(c)}(t)
	-i[H,\rho^{(c)}(t)]\,dt
	-\frac{\kappa}{2}\{c^\dagger c,\rho^{(c)}(t)\}\,dt
	+O(dt^2).
\end{align}
The normalization factor is
\begin{align}
	\Tr\!\left[\tilde\rho^{(c)}_0(t+dt)\right]
	&=
	1-\kappa\,\Tr\!\left[c^\dagger c\,\rho^{(c)}(t)\right]dt+O(dt^2)
	=
	1-\kappa\langle c^\dagger c\rangle_t\,dt+O(dt^2).
\end{align}
Therefore, dividing by the trace and keeping terms up to $O(dt)$,
\begin{align}
	\rho^{(c)}_0(t+dt)
	&=
	\rho^{(c)}(t)
	-i[H,\rho^{(c)}(t)]\,dt
	-\frac{\kappa}{2}\{c^\dagger c,\rho^{(c)}(t)\}\,dt
	+\kappa\langle c^\dagger c\rangle_t\,\rho^{(c)}(t)\,dt
	+O(dt^2).
	\label{eq:noclick_update_expanded}
\end{align}
It is convenient to introduce the superoperator (following Ref.)
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal{H}[A]\rho
	=
	A\rho+\rho A^\dagger-\Tr[(A+A^\dagger)\rho]\;\rho,
	\label{eq:H_superop}
\end{equation}
so that for $A=c^\dagger c$ (Hermitian) one has
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal{H}[c^\dagger c]\rho
	=
	\{c^\dagger c,\rho\}-2\langle c^\dagger c\rangle\,\rho.
\end{equation}
Equation \eqref{eq:noclick_update_expanded} can then be rewritten as
\begin{equation}
	\rho^{(c)}_0(t+dt)
	=
	\rho^{(c)}(t)
	-i[H,\rho^{(c)}(t)]\,dt
	-\frac{\kappa}{2}\,\mathcal{H}[c^\dagger c]\rho^{(c)}(t)\,dt
	+O(dt^2).
	\label{eq:noclick_update_Hform}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection*{Click update}

From Eq.~\eqref{eq:unnorm_click} and Eq.~\eqref{eq:prob_click}, the normalized post-click state is
\begin{equation}
	\rho^{(c)}_1(t+dt)
	=
	\frac{c\,\rho^{(c)}(t)\,c^\dagger}{\Tr[c\,\rho^{(c)}(t)\,c^\dagger]}
	=
	\frac{c\,\rho^{(c)}(t)\,c^\dagger}{\langle c^\dagger c\rangle_t}.
	\label{eq:click_update}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection*{Stochastic master equation (jump SME)}

We introduce the Poisson increment $dN_t\in\{0,1\}$ describing whether a click occurred in $[t,t+dt)$.
Its conditional mean is
\begin{equation}
	\mathbb{E}[dN_t]=p_1(t+dt)=\kappa\,\langle c^\dagger c\rangle_t\,dt.
	\label{eq:poisson_mean}
\end{equation}
The conditional increment $d\rho^{(c)}(t)=\rho^{(c)}(t+dt)-\rho^{(c)}(t)$ can be written by combining the two
branches:
\begin{align}
	d\rho^{(c)}(t)
	&=
	dN_t\big(\rho^{(c)}_1(t+dt)-\rho^{(c)}(t)\big)
	+(1-dN_t)\big(\rho^{(c)}_0(t+dt)-\rho^{(c)}(t)\big).
\end{align}
Discarding terms of order $dt\,dN_t$ (higher than $dt$ in It\^o counting), and using
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:noclick_update_Hform} and \eqref{eq:click_update}, we obtain the jump SME
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
		d\rho^{(c)}
		=
		-i[H,\rho^{(c)}]\,dt
		-\frac{\kappa}{2}\,\mathcal{H}[c^\dagger c]\rho^{(c)}\,dt
		+
		\left(
		\frac{c\rho^{(c)}c^\dagger}{\Tr[c\rho^{(c)}c^\dagger]}-\rho^{(c)}
		\right)dN_t.}
	\label{eq:SME_jump_single}
\end{equation}

The unconditional state is recovered by averaging Eq.~\eqref{eq:SME_jump_single} over the Poisson increment.
Using
\begin{equation}
	\mathbb{E}\!\left[dN_t\,f(\rho^{(c)})\right]
	=
	\kappa\,dt\;
	\mathbb{E}\!\left[\Tr(c^\dagger c\,\rho^{(c)})\,f(\rho^{(c)})\right],
\end{equation}
one obtains
\begin{equation}
	\mathbb{E}[d\rho^{(c)}]
	=
	-i[H,\rho]\,dt
	+\kappa\,\mathcal{D}[c]\rho\,dt,
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the Lindblad master equation \eqref{eq:Lindblad_single_channel}.

\subsubsection*{Many channels}

For multiple monitored channels $\{c_k\}$ (each with rate $\kappa_k$) we introduce independent Poisson increments
$dN_k(t)\in\{0,1\}$ with
\begin{equation}
	\mathbb{E}[dN_k(t)]=\kappa_k\,\Tr[c_k^\dagger c_k\,\rho^{(c)}(t)]dt,
\end{equation}
and the SME becomes
\begin{equation}
	d\rho^{(c)}
	=
	-i[H,\rho^{(c)}]\,dt
	-\frac{1}{2}\sum_k \kappa_k\,\mathcal{H}[c_k^\dagger c_k]\rho^{(c)}\,dt
	+\sum_k
	\left(
	\frac{c_k\rho^{(c)}c_k^\dagger}{\Tr[c_k\rho^{(c)}c_k^\dagger]}-\rho^{(c)}
	\right)dN_k.
	\label{eq:SME_jump_multi}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Stochastic Schr\"odinger equation and quantum trajectories}

If the initial state is pure and the monitoring is perfect, the conditional state remains pure at all times.
One can then rewrite the jump dynamics as a stochastic Schr\"odinger equation (SSE) for the conditional state vector
$\ket{\psi^{(c)}(t)}$.
For a single channel, we know that
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
		d\ket{\psi^{(c)}(t)}
		=
		\left[
		-iH\,dt
		+\frac{\kappa}{2}\big(\langle c^\dagger c\rangle_t-c^\dagger c\big)dt
		+\left(\frac{c}{\sqrt{\langle c^\dagger c\rangle_t}}-\mathbb{I}\right)dN_t
		\right]\ket{\psi^{(c)}(t)}.}
	\label{eq:SSE_jump_single}
\end{equation}
Solutions of SSEs are often called \emph{quantum trajectories}.
Averaging the projectors $\ket{\psi^{(c)}(t)}\!\bra{\psi^{(c)}(t)}$ over many trajectories reproduces the
solution of the unconditional master equation.

\subsection{Inefficient detection and partial unravelling}

The previous derivations assumed an ideal measurement of the environment.
A simple and widely used model for finite efficiency $\eta\in[0,1]$ is that the measurement succeeds with
probability $\eta$ and fails with probability $1-\eta$.
Equivalently, one can place a fictitious beam splitter of transmissivity $\eta$ before the detector,
so that a fraction of the output is lost.

A key conceptual point is that inefficient monitoring can be
described by \emph{unravelling only a part} of the unconditional generator.
Indeed, one may rewrite the unconditional Lindbladian as a sum of two identical dissipators weighted by $\eta$ and
$1-\eta$:
\begin{equation}
	\frac{d\rho(t)}{dt}
	=
	\eta\kappa\,\mathcal{D}[c]\rho(t)
	+
	(1-\eta)\kappa\,\mathcal{D}[c]\rho(t).
	\label{eq:split_dissipator_eta}
\end{equation}
The first term is interpreted as the \emph{monitored} part of the dynamics, while the second term is the
\emph{unmonitored} (lost) part.
One then unravels only the monitored term, while the unmonitored one remains as a deterministic drift in the SME.

For photodetection, the resulting inefficient jump SME reads Ref.
\begin{equation}
	\boxed{
		d\rho^{(c)}
		=
		-i[H,\rho^{(c)}]\,dt
		-\frac{\eta\kappa}{2}\,\mathcal{H}[c^\dagger c]\rho^{(c)}\,dt
		+
		\left(
		\frac{c\rho^{(c)}c^\dagger}{\Tr[c\rho^{(c)}c^\dagger]}-\rho^{(c)}
		\right)dN_t
		+
		(1-\eta)\kappa\,\mathcal{D}[c]\rho^{(c)}\,dt,}
	\label{eq:SME_jump_inefficient}
\end{equation}
where now the Poisson increment has reduced mean
\begin{equation}
	\mathbb{E}[dN_t]=\eta\kappa\,\Tr[c^\dagger c\,\rho^{(c)}(t)]\,dt.
	\label{eq:poisson_mean_ineff}
\end{equation}
For $\eta<1$ the conditional state is generally mixed, because the unobserved term
$(1-\eta)\kappa\,\mathcal{D}[c]$ produces decoherence that cannot be purified by conditioning on the detected record.
This provides a clear operational meaning of ``unravelling only a piece'' of the differential equation:
only the monitored part gives rise to stochastic state updates correlated with the measurement record,
while the remaining unmonitored contribution stays as a deterministic Lindblad drift.
